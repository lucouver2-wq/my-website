The framework

Three limits, four research answers.

Optical astronomy is limited by atmosphere, optics, and noise. The four works below show how computational imaging turns each limit into a measurable, correctable, or learnable signal.

Work 1 / Nature Photonics 2024

Directly observing atmospheric turbulence.

A video-rate wide-field wavefront sensor makes turbulence visible across the field of view, measuring how wavefronts evolve before they blur astronomical images.

Scientific idea

Ground-based telescopes are limited by atmospheric turbulence. This work demonstrates a wide-field sensing strategy that records wavefront variation over time, helping astronomers understand where, when, and how seeing degrades the image.

video-rate sensing wide field adaptive optics precursor

Work 2 / Nature 2022

A sensor that makes imperfect optics computational.

The integrated meta-imaging sensor captures information that conventional cameras discard, enabling digital aberration correction and 3D refocusing after the exposure.

Why it matters

Instead of building a perfect optical system, the instrument measures richer optical information and leaves part of the image formation problem to computation.

Work 3 / ASTERIS

Use ASTERIS like an astronomer.

ASTERIS is a self-supervised spatiotemporal denoising framework for astronomical imaging. It uses aligned exposures to learn correlated noise and improve faint-source detection while preserving photometric and PSF fidelity.

01

Align exposures

Start from reduced frames registered to a common WCS and grouped by pointing.

02

Split by signal regime

Bright pixels keep dynamic range; faint regions become the learning target.

03

Denoise and validate

Compare completeness, purity, photometry, and PSF fidelity before trusting candidates.
